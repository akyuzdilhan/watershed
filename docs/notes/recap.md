# Post-match recap and audience previews (`worker.recap`)

## What

`src/worker/recap.py` turns the desk of a finished match (`cards.json` and `state.json` as written by `JsonFileSink`, or the documents of the match in Cosmos DB) into a recap: a deterministic analysis, then three texts per language (`en`, `fr`, `es`, the last labelled "AI-written, not reviewed"). `recap_async` (or `build_recap`, with the storyline for the table) is called by the worker at the final whistle (`docs/notes/worker-io.md`, "Recap at the final whistle"), which stores the result locally as `recap.json` in the match folder of the desk, in the format the command line writes, and in Cosmos DB as a document of type `recap` with id `recap-<match_id>`, for the console's "After the match" section. The command line does the same for a desk already written.

- **Input.** Only `approved` cards are read. Retracted, rejected and pending cards are never cited. The points of `wp_timeline` that belong to a retracted moment are dropped: a voided goal leaves a one-point spike on the live curve (63' and 69' in the demo).
- **Analysis** (`analyse`). Final and half-time score (the half-time score is given only when the approved goals add up to the final score), the turning point (the approved goal or red card that raised the eventual winner's chance of winning the most; in a draw, the one that moved either side's chance the most), the 3 biggest moments by `value_pts`, a per-team summary (goals, red cards, scorers with minutes, saves and cards on air), the key points of the curve (kick-off, half-time, the winner's lowest point, the minute from which the winner stayed above 50% for good, final), and the goals VAR ruled out. Win probabilities at moments come from the card facts (as aired, clamped by `shown`); the other side's is 100 minus the card side's and the draw.
- **Facts** (`recap_facts`). A flat dict with explicit field names (`varleigh_lowest_wp_pct`, `turning_point_varleigh_wp_after_pct`, `disallowed_1_offside_call`...) plus their meanings, like `card_facts`. `value_pts` only orders the biggest moments and is not a fact; league table points are not facts either: "points" in a text is only a gap between two chances.
- **Goals ruled out by VAR** (`disallowed_goals`). Read from the retracted goal cards: the reason and the call (tight or clear) from the worker's `var_decision` first, so that the call survives a store that keeps no feed events, else from the desk's `ruled_out`; the margin only from `ruled_out.margin_cm`, for the producer: `var_decision.margin_m` is never copied into the recap document, stored next to the cards. The texts say "offside by a whisker" or "clear offside" ("hors-jeu d'un rien", "hors-jeu net"; "fuera de juego por muy poco", "fuera de juego claro"), never the margin.
- **The table** (`season_facts`, from `storyline.post_match`). For the club whose chance of going down moved by 3 points or more (the winner first), the rank once the whole matchday is played: "Varleigh move from 17th to 16th once the matchday is played", "Après la journée, Varleigh est 16e (17e avant le match)", "Tras la jornada, el Varleigh queda 16.º". When only the rank with this result alone is known, it is told as provisional ("provisionally 14th", "provisoirement 14e", "provisionalmente 14.º"). With the rank after the matchday, the rank and the drop-zone flag with this result alone are left out of the facts, so the model cannot contradict it.
- **Texts.** `recap` (max 100 words, neutral), `fan` (max 50, emotional, from one side: the winner by default, `--fan-team` to change; a draw is "honours even", never "a point"), `analyst` (max 60, numbers-led, following the winner, or home in a draw). French without an article before a club and with no-break spaces before : ; ? ! and %; Spanish with the article ("el Varleigh", "llevó al Varleigh"); minutes as "à la 66'" in French.
- **Writers.** `RecapWriter`: an Agent Framework agent that calls the same model on Foundry, same set-up as `FoundryWriter` with its own effort and output budget (`FOUNDRY_RECAP_REASONING_EFFORT`, `FOUNDRY_RECAP_MAX_TOKENS`: low and 6,000 in the templates), with a `get_recap_facts(match_id)` function tool. Its instructions follow the templates: the word limits are the Verifier's, the offside example is "offside by a whisker", never a margin, "points" never goes with a verb of league points (take, gain, win, earn, pick up; prendre, gagner, empocher; ganar, sumar, conseguir) and a draw is never "a point", the table is provisional unless the rank after the matchday is given, no em dash. `RecapTemplateWriter`: fixed sentences, each used only when every field it names is in the facts; trailing sentences are dropped if a text goes over its limit. Flow: model, one retry (with the list of failures as a note), then the template.
- **Verifier** (`verify_recap`). Runs `writer.verify` on each text (every number claimed, claims match their field, % from `_pct` fields, no decimal probability), then adds: length per section (French "44 %" is one word), `names_ok` must be empty (in `writer.verify` it is reported but not blocking), no player of the match whose name is not in the facts, no spelled-out numbers (two/deux/dos...), no minute of a retracted card outside a sentence that says VAR or ruled out, and the on-air rules (`writer.air_issues`: no verb of league points with "points", no league-wide rate, ratio, bits or count of matches, no em dash; an em dash in a reply is first normalised to a comma).

On the demo, the turning point is the substitute's 66' goal (Varleigh 28% to 79%, 51 points), the biggest moments are 66', 57' and 14', and the texts are verified in the three languages.

## How to run

```
python -m worker data/replay/demo-0007.jsonl.gz --writer template --auto-approve --desk-dir data/desk
python -m worker.recap data/desk/demo-0007                       # template writer, writes data/desk/demo-0007/recap.json and prints it
python -m worker.recap data/desk/demo-0007 --writer foundry      # needs FOUNDRY_PROJECT_ENDPOINT and FOUNDRY_MODEL (.env); paid calls
python -m worker.recap data/desk/demo-0007 --fan-team away --out recap-yarnford.json
python -m worker.recap --cosmos demo-0007                        # reads cards and match state from Cosmos DB, upserts the recap next to them
```

Tests: `pytest tests/test_recap.py` (template writer only; scripted model replies check the retry, the on-air rules and the fallback).

## What is imperfect

- **Cosmos mode of the command line.** In Azure the worker writes the recap through `CosmosSink` (the reference replay of 26/10 stored it); `recap_cosmos`, behind `python -m worker.recap --cosmos`, writes the same document with `type: "recap"` into the `cards` container and has no test against Cosmos DB. A reader of that container must filter on `type`.
- **Foundry path, live.** On the reference replay of 26/10 the recap agent's two drafts failed the Verifier (numbers without a claim, then an invalid reply) and the template wrote the recap (`docs/evaluation.md`, "Live run, production prompt"). An earlier local pass at medium effort and 3,000 tokens was cut while reasoning, hence low effort and 6,000 tokens. The 100/50/60 word limits are tight for French with three texts in one call.
- **Turning point = goals and red cards only.** A save or a missed chance does not move the win probability; it can appear in the biggest moments (by `value_pts`, which is counterfactual) but never as the turning point.
- **Rounding.** The opponent's chance at a moment is 100 minus two rounded values: it can be 1 point off the curve.
- **The rank after the matchday** comes from the synthetic league, which plays the other matches of the matchday; with a real feed, the final whistle of this match comes before the others and the provisional sentence would be the honest one.
- **Name check is heuristic.** `names_ok` flags capitalised words that are not in the facts and not at the start of a sentence; a correct word that happens to be capitalised mid-sentence fails the draft and sends it to the retry, then the template (VAR, "Premier League", the days and the months are exempt: `COMMON_CAPITALISED`). Players are only known from the cards, so a player with no card at all cannot be detected by `unknown_players` (`names_ok` still catches him).
- **Spelled-out numbers** are checked from a small word list; "hat-trick", "a brace" or "ten men" (blocked) are crude edges.
- **Templates.** Simple and repetitive ("Varleigh 5-2 Yarnford" right after "for Varleigh"), no gender agreement in French (`Varleigh est tombé`). The fan text in a draw can read flat after a late equaliser.
- **Half-time** is detected as the clock going back by more than 120 s; a match stopped before the second half has no half-time point.
- **`writer.verify` reuse.** It hard-codes `headline`/`why` and the card lengths, so each recap text is passed as a `headline` and its `length_ok` is ignored. A cleaner split would be a `verify_text(body, claims, facts)` helper in `writer.py` that both use.
